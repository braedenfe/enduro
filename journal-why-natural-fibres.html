Why we choose natural fibres.

We’ve never believed performance has to come from plastic.

Enduro · For the long run

Look through any Enduro garment and you’ll find natural fibres at the core. Australian merino wool and cotton. Fibres that have been refining themselves for millions of years.

If you’ve ever wondered why we don’t build our apparel from polyester, here’s the simple answer.

Because we don’t think plastic belongs against your skin.

Most activewear is made from it. Polyester, nylon and elastane all come from petroleum, and they sit against your skin while you move, sweat and heat up. That is exactly when your body may be most exposed to what’s in them.

What you’re not wearing
01Microplastics+

Synthetic activewear sheds tiny plastic fibres every time you wear and wash it. Microplastics have now been found in human blood, lungs and placentas. When you’re hot, sweating and breathing hard, you may be absorbing and inhaling more of them. Early research has linked microplastics to inflammation, and many carry chemicals known to disrupt the endocrine system and interfere with your hormones.

02PFAS, the forever chemicals+

PFAS are used to make fabrics water and stain resistant. They break down so slowly they build up in the body over time, and they’ve been linked to effects on the immune system, hormones and fertility, and an increased risk of some cancers. Independent testing has found signs of PFAS in a number of popular sports bras and leggings. Australia has now banned three of the most widely studied.

03Hormone disruption+

Some chemicals found in synthetic activewear can mimic or interfere with your hormones. In 2022, US testing found BPA, a known endocrine disruptor, in polyester and spandex sports bras and athletic shirts from major brands, at levels up to 22 times California’s safety limit. BPA can be absorbed through the skin, and warm, sweaty skin may take in more.

Every Enduro piece is PFAS free. Our merino is Australian, and our shorts are made from GOTS certified organic cotton.

What you’ll notice

Natural fibres don’t just leave things out. They feel different from the first wear.

Drier, not damp

Polyester can’t absorb sweat, so it sits on your skin and the fabric sticks to you almost straight away. Merino and cotton draw moisture in and let it breathe out, so you stay drier and more comfortable for longer.

Breathes with you

Merino and cotton let air and heat move through the fabric instead of trapping them against your body the way plastic does. Merino goes a step further, helping regulate your temperature as you warm up and cool down.

Fresh for longer

Polyester is known for holding onto odour, even after washing. Merino and cotton don’t cling to it the same way, and merino naturally resists odour, so you can train more and wash less.

Soft against your skin

Our ultra fine merino is soft with no itch, and our organic cotton is breathable and gets softer with every wash. No plastic sheen, no static, nothing sticking to you.

On durability

People often point to polyester because it’s durable. And they’re right.

It’s so durable that long after you’re finished with it, it’ll likely still exist somewhere as plastic waste.

We’d rather make apparel that’s built to last through years of training, not centuries in landfill.

Natural fibres soften. They develop character. They tell the story of every run, every workout, every adventure you’ve taken them on, and at the end of their life they biodegrade. Treat them well, follow the care instructions, and they’ll reward you with years of performance.

“What about recycled polyester?”

It’s certainly better than creating new plastic.

But it’s still plastic.

It still sheds microplastics as you wear and wash it. It still doesn’t biodegrade. And eventually, it reaches the same destination as virgin polyester.

We’re not interested in making slightly better plastic activewear. We’re interested in proving you don’t need plastic in the first place.

The science is still evolving. But we’d rather not wait decades to find out.

The best performance material was never engineered in a laboratory.
It was engineered by nature.
