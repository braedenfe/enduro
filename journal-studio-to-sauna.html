Studio to sauna.

Two pieces in Australian merino, built to be worn from a Pilates class through a run and into the sauna without changing.

25 September 2026 · Enduro

Most women's activewear is built for one activity and one temperature. You change for the studio, change again for a run, and change again before the sauna. Each garment does its narrow job and none of them handle the gaps.

The Micro Short and the Bandeau were designed as a set, in one fabric, to cover the whole day.

Why merino handles the range

It regulates in both directions, which synthetics do not.

Merino absorbs moisture vapour into the fibre before it becomes liquid sweat, and releases heat as it does. The practical effect is a garment that feels warm as conditions cool and breathable as they warm.

That range is the whole point here. A Pilates studio at 22 degrees, a run in the sun, and a sauna well above 80 are three very different environments. A synthetic that wicks fast in one will feel clammy in the next and cold the moment you stop.

Seamless, because seams are what rub

Both pieces are knitted, not cut and sewn.

The Micro Short is a seamless rib knit with a mid-rise waistband. The Bandeau is a seamless double-layer knit that gives light, secure support without underwire or hardware. No side seams means nothing to dig in through a reformer class, and nothing to chafe over distance.

A 100% merino lining

The gusset is pure merino, not a synthetic liner.

Almost every short on the market, including natural fibre ones, lines the gusset in polyester. It is cheaper and easier to work with. It also means the one part of the garment in constant contact with the most sensitive skin on your body is plastic.

We line the Micro Short with 100% merino so only natural fibre sits there. Merino is breathable, absorbs moisture into the fibre rather than holding it against the skin, and naturally resists the bacteria responsible for odour. For a garment worn through sweat and heat, that matters more here than anywhere else in the range.

Why fabric choice matters in a sauna

Heat changes how synthetic fabric behaves.

Polyester does not melt at sauna temperatures. It does soften. The glass transition point of polyester sits between roughly 67 and 81 degrees, which overlaps the range most saunas run at. A softened polymer is more prone to releasing what is held inside it.

Synthetic fabrics shed microscopic fibres through wear, and those fibres carry the chemical additives used in their manufacture. Research using human skin models has shown that some of these additives can cross the skin barrier and reach the bloodstream, and that hydrated skin absorbs more than dry skin.

A sauna is the most hydrated your skin will ever be. Several of the chemical classes involved are recognised endocrine disruptors. We would rather not hold plastic against the skin at 90 degrees and find out what that means over years.

Wool has been worn in saunas for centuries. It breathes, handles moisture without clinging, and is a protein fibre rather than a plastic one. Both pieces are rated from 20 degrees through to sauna heat, which is why we built the set around a single fabric.
